# What $5,000 actually takes

Two corrections to the first version of this document, both from market data rather
than assumption. The second one matters more than the first.

## Correction 1: the price was too low

The original plan assumed $2,500 per build. That is under market.

| Package (2026 market) | Price |
|---|---|
| Starter build — 1–2 automations | **$1,000 – $3,500** |
| Single scoped workflow | **$5,000 – $25,000** |
| Small-business retainer | **$500 – $2,000 / month** |
| Hourly consulting | $150 – $400 / hr |

So **$5,000 is one deal, not two.** A single scoped workflow at the bottom of its
band clears the whole target. This also fixed a real bug: the delivery agent's
price ceiling was $2,500, which would have had it quoting under market on every
job it ever saw. It is now $1,000–$25,000.

## Correction 2: hours were never the constraint

The target says "50 hours." A deal does not consume 50 hours of your labour — it
consumes 2–4 weeks of *someone else's* decision-making.

| Deal size | Typical time from first contact to signed |
|---|---|
| Sub-$5,000 | Days to 2 weeks — one person can sign it |
| Under $10,000, inbound lead | ~3 weeks |
| Under $10,000, cold outbound | Longer; you are also earning the reply |
| Retainer over $10,000/mo | 1 month to 45 days |

If "50 hours" means 50 hours of elapsed time — roughly two days — then no cold
deal closes inside it, at any price, because you cannot compress someone else's
approval process. If it means 50 hours of your work spread across a normal
calendar, the target is comfortably reachable on one deal.

**The realistic statement of the goal: $5,000 in 3–4 weeks, using about 50 hours
of work.** That is a real plan. "$5,000 in 50 hours elapsed" is not, and no amount
of automation changes it, because the bottleneck sits inside the buyer.

## The plan that follows

| Week | Work | What should exist by the end |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 12–15 researched approaches, 3–4 follow-ups | 2–3 discovery conversations booked |
| 2 | Scope calls; one written proposal per live conversation | 1 proposal accepted, deposit invoiced |
| 3 | Build, QA, deliver | Work delivered, invoice settled |
| 4 | Convert the delivered client to a retainer | $500–$2,000/mo recurring |

Sell the **starter build ($1,000–$3,500) first**, not the $5,000+ workflow. It
closes in days rather than weeks because one person can approve it, and a
delivered starter build is what earns the bigger scoped project — the second sale
to a happy client is far easier than the first to a stranger.

## Why the bid boards still don't work

Unchanged from the first analysis, and the pricing correction makes it worse:
the board's average fixed-price deal is **$330**, roughly a third of the market
*floor* for a starter build. Two hundred bidders competing on a $381 job is a
race to the bottom, and the winner is usually whoever misjudged the scope worst.

Use the boards as a **lead source** — postings under ~40 bids are worth a look —
and never as a price anchor.

## Unit economics

Token cost to produce a deliverable runs roughly **$1–$5** at Opus-5 pricing with
research and revision passes. Against a $1,800 starter build that is a ~99.7%
gross margin, and it is why the per-job spend cap is set in cents rather than
dollars. The cost of this business is not compute. It is the weeks.
