# The outreach that fits this offer

Written for direct outreach, not marketplace bidding. The arithmetic behind that
choice is in `docs/channel-math.md` — the short version is that the bid boards
need 400–3,000 bids to reach $5,000, and direct outreach needs seven conversations.

## Why you can write a strong pitch today

Most people selling "AI automation" have a deck. You have a running system with
an audit trail: a firm of six agents, a ledger that only counts settled payments,
approval gates on every outbound action, and 34 tests covering the money path.

That is the pitch. Not "I can build you an AI agent" — *"here is one running, and
here is what it refuses to do, which is why you can trust it with your invoices."*

## The shape

Four sentences. No preamble, no credentials paragraph, no "hope this finds you well."

1. **The specific thing you noticed** about *their* business — not about AI.
2. **What you'd build**, in their vocabulary, in one sentence.
3. **The price and the timebox.** A number, not a range.
4. **One question** that is easy to answer.

## Worked example

> Subject: your quote desk
>
> You've had two "quote desk coordinator" roles open since June — I'm guessing
> quotes are still going out by hand and the 3-day turnaround is costing you deals.
>
> I build agents that own one job end to end. For your quote desk that means:
> reads the inbound request, pulls the rates, drafts the quote, sends it for one
> human approval, then chases the follow-up if nobody replies in 48 hours.
>
> $2,500, running in your inbox within a week. You keep the approval step, so
> nothing reaches a customer that you haven't seen.
>
> Is the 3-day turnaround actually the problem, or is it something else?

## Why each part works

- **Opens on their problem, sourced.** The open roles are checkable. That single
  observable fact is what separates this from the 200 other messages they get.
- **Describes the job, not the technology.** No one buys "an agentic workflow."
  They buy "quotes go out same day."
- **The approval step is the sale, not a caveat.** Their real objection is "I
  don't trust software to email my customers." Answer it before they raise it.
- **One price, one timebox.** A range invites negotiation before there's interest.
- **The question is easy and non-committal.** You want a reply, not a yes.

## What not to do

- Don't send it to anyone you have nothing specific to say about. A generic
  version of this is worse than silence — it burns the contact permanently.
- Don't attach a deck. The email is the deck.
- Don't claim clients, results, or numbers you don't have. One unverifiable
  claim costs you the whole message.
- Don't follow up more than twice. Two is persistence; four is a reason to block.

## Where to find the seven

You need roughly seven real conversations to close two builds at $2,500.
Ranked by how warm they start:

1. **Businesses you or your circle already know.** Highest close rate of anything
   here, and the only channel with no competition at all.
2. **Local businesses with a visible manual bottleneck** — a booking phone line,
   a quote desk, an inbox that answers slowly. You can observe the bottleneck
   yourself, which gives you the sourced opening line.
3. **Companies hiring for the job you'd automate.** An open coordinator role is
   public evidence that the work is manual and that they've already budgeted for
   it. Job boards are a lead source here, not a bidding arena.
4. **Marketplace postings under ~40 bids**, and only those. See the channel math.
