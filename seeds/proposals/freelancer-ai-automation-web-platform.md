# Bid — "AI Automation for Web Platform"

**Posting:** https://www.freelancer.com/projects/ai-automation/automation-for-web-platform
**Budget:** ₹12,500–37,500 · **Average bid:** ₹23,278 · **37 bids** · closes in ~3 days
**Read before sending:** [docs/where-to-sell.md](../../docs/where-to-sell.md) — this is a
₹-denominated board, so treat it as portfolio-building, not as the business.

## What the posting actually asks for

Quoted from the listing, because the bid has to answer these and most won't:

- Discovery session to identify high-impact automation opportunities
- Automation logic — ML or rule-based
- API integration with an existing web application
- Cloud deployment with monitoring
- Documentation and a knowledge-transfer call
- **Acceptance:** runs end-to-end in the app, consistent results, **handles edge
  cases gracefully, and comes with tests**

Stack: Python/Flask or Node/Express, open to alternatives. AWS, GCP or Azure.
They gave no business context and said they want speed and clarity.

## The angle

Thirty-seven bidders will promise speed. The acceptance criteria ask for
**edge cases and tests** — that is the client quietly saying they have been burned
by something that demoed well and broke in production. Answer that, and the bid
stops competing on price.

Bid at **₹34,000** — near the top of their range, below it only if you want the
work more than the rate. Do not bid ₹12,500; on this board the low bid signals
you did not read the acceptance criteria.

## The bid

> You've asked for tests and graceful edge-case handling, which tells me you've
> had an automation demo well and then fail quietly in production. That's the part
> I build for first.
>
> **How I'd run it:**
>
> **Discovery (day 1)** — one call to find the workflow that actually costs you
> money, and to agree what "working" means as a checkable condition, not a
> feeling. You get that written down before I build anything.
>
> **Build (days 2–5)** — Python/Flask, integrated through your existing API. Rule
> based where the logic is deterministic, model-driven only where it genuinely
> needs judgement — mixing those up is what makes automations unpredictable.
>
> **Tests (throughout)** — every edge case we identify in discovery becomes a test
> that fails before the fix and passes after. You get the test suite, so you can
> verify the thing works without taking my word for it.
>
> **Deploy + handover (days 6–7)** — your cloud, with monitoring and alerting on
> failures. Documentation written for whoever maintains it after me, plus the
> knowledge-transfer call.
>
> **₹34,000, delivered in 7 days.**
>
> One question so I can scope this properly: which workflow is costing you the
> most right now — and roughly how many times a day does it run?

## Why each part works

- **Opens on their stated acceptance criteria**, not on my credentials. It proves
  the posting was read, which most of 37 bids will not.
- **Names the actual failure mode** (demos well, fails quietly). If that's true
  for them, it reads as recognition rather than a pitch.
- **"You get the test suite, so you can verify without taking my word for it"** —
  removes the trust problem that makes clients pick the cheapest bid.
- **Day-by-day plan** answers "speed and clarity" concretely instead of claiming it.
- **One question at the end**, easy to answer, and its answer is what you need to
  scope accurately.

## Do not

- Do not attach a portfolio PDF. The bid is the portfolio.
- Do not claim past clients or results you do not have. One unverifiable claim
  loses the bid against 36 others.
- Do not bid on the 200-bid postings on the same board. Check the count first.
